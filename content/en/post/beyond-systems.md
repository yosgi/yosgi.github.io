Recently, I noticed that some companies have started offering services to build a digital twin of a person — usually in the form of a 3D avatar that looks like you and can interact with others on your behalf. It’s an interesting idea, but to be honest, it’s not exactly what I expect.


The simulated faces often look a bit unnatural, and they don’t really feel like the person they’re supposed to represent. So I decided to take a different approach.


Instead of chasing visual realism, I wanted to create something that captures my thought process and personality — not my face.


So I chose to use WALL·E, the little robot from the Disney movie, as my front-end character. It’s cute, emotional, and expressive — perfect for representing a digital version of “me.”


Here’s how it works:

- WALL·E acts as the front-end agent that interacts with people.
- Behind the scenes, ChatGPT serves as the reasoning engine.
- It connects directly to my Notion workspace, which stores my thoughts, documents, and knowledge.

Over time, my Notion becomes more like my external brain, a living memory that WALL·E can draw upon.


When someone talks to WALL·E, it uses MCP2 to interact with the data, respond intelligently, and even express reactions — as if it were me.


The more I document in Notion, the more “alive” WALL·E becomes — more consistent, more contextual, and more me
