## I. Core Strategy: Bet the Dip

In 2025, my primary investment strategy was **Bet the Dip**—buying into positions when market sentiment turned distinctly pessimistic and prices showed temporary dislocations, rather than chasing short-term momentum.

In practice, the majority of my positions were established during **the sharp correction in April, a smaller pullback in September, and the systemic sell-off in AI-related assets in November**.

These periods shared a common characteristic: while short-term news flow was largely negative, I did not observe a fundamental breakdown in the long-term AI investment thesis. Based on this assessment, I chose to take contrarian positions during these drawdowns.

## II. Overall Results: Returns Driven by Judgment and Entry Timing

From a full-year perspective, as of year-end:

- **Total Realized PnL:** $2,171
- **Win Rate:** 77.78%
- **Expectancy (per trade):** +$60.31
- **Average Holding Period:** 36.7 days
These metrics indicate that the year’s performance was not driven by isolated, high-risk bets, but rather by repeated decisions with **a relatively high win rate and positive expectancy**.

## III. Trade Structure: High Win Rate, Not a High-Payout Strategy

Looking more closely at trade-level statistics:

- **Profit Factor:** 2.69
- **Payoff Ratio:** 0.77 (average win / average loss)
This profile suggests that my strategy is more **win-rate-driven** than **payoff-driven**. In other words, most profitable trades generated small to moderate gains, while a limited number of losing trades resulted in comparatively larger losses.

This structure is not inherently flawed, but it does require **stricter position sizing and clearer risk isolation**, particularly when mistakes occur.

## IV. Best-Performing Trades: Trend Alignment and Time in Position

The strongest contributors to performance were concentrated in **Rocket Lab (RKLB)** and **NVIDIA (NVDA)**:

- **Rocket Lab (RKLB)**
- **NVIDIA (NVDA)**
These trades shared several common features:

- Entries were made near local or cyclical lows
- Positions were given sufficient time to develop
- Short-term volatility did not prematurely force exits
This further validates the effectiveness of **Bet the Dip combined with medium-term holding** in trend-driven assets.

## V. Major Mistake: Extreme Loss from a Speculative Trade

The largest loss of the year came from a **GME put option trade**:

- Single-trade loss of approximately **$584**
- Return close to **100%**
- Essentially a **high-risk, short-term speculative position**
The core issue was not directional judgment, but rather **a complete imbalance in position sizing**. Given that my overall strategy relies on a high win rate, the presence of an extreme loss had a disproportionate negative impact on overall performance.

This stands out as the most important and unambiguous lesson of the year.

## VI. Other Losing Trades: Within Tolerance, but Worth Reviewing

Beyond GME, other notable losses included:

- **CRCL:** approximately **18.7%**
- **AAPL:** approximately **10.8%**
These losses fall closer to a **normal error range** in trading, but they nonetheless highlight the need for more conservative sizing when trend confirmation is incomplete.

## VII. Long-Term View on AI Investments

I continue to believe that **AI is better approached from a medium- to long-term investment perspective**.

While large-scale consumer (2C) applications remain limited in the near term, AI adoption in **enterprise (2B) use cases**—particularly in compute, infrastructure, and enterprise software—still shows strong long-term potential.

Given my professional background in **AI application development**, I am inclined to believe that meaningful commercialization and value realization are more likely to emerge **from 2026 onward**. This conviction underpins my willingness to tolerate short-term volatility and deploy medium-sized positions during periods of market weakness.

## VIII. Annual Summary: Clear Structure, Tighter Risk Boundaries Needed

Overall, my 2025 investment performance exhibited several defining characteristics:

**What worked well**

- Ability to buy into positions during periods of pessimistic market sentiment
- Patience in holding trend-driven assets
- A strategy supported by positive expectancy
**Areas for improvement**

- Lack of strict position caps for speculative trades
- Increased sensitivity to extreme losses under a win-rate-driven framework
- Insufficient differentiation between long-term core holdings and short-term profit-taking rules
